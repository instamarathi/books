Imagine two offers.

Offer A pays 50,000 rupees a month and is stable. Offer B has a higher upside: some months it may pay 1,20,000, but in the bad months it may pay nothing at all. On paper, Offer B looks better if you only calculate the average. In real life, a family in Lucknow, Ranchi, or Surat will often choose Offer A. Why? Because the bad months are not abstract. They are the months when a father's medicines, a younger sibling's fees, or a rent payment still have to be made.

This is the logic behind Poor Economics. The poor are not irrational. They are living too close to ruin to treat average return as the whole story.

**When failure is catastrophic, the real problem is not low upside. It is high variance.**

That is why many Indian households do not reject risk. They reject unprotected risk. They will let a son or daughter prepare for civil services while working a regular job. They will tolerate a side business so long as the salary continues. They will even praise ambition if the downside has already been covered by someone else.

Scarcity adds another layer. When every bill is pressing for attention, the mind becomes narrower. Long-term planning is not impossible; it is crowded out. A family that is constantly juggling school fees, rent, and medical expenses does not get to think like a portfolio manager. It thinks like a firewall.

## Use these 5 techniques

1. **Calculate the downside first.** Before discussing upside, ask what a bad month, a bad year, or a failed attempt would actually do to the household.
2. **Prefer protected bets.** If someone wants to explore a new field, look for ways to cap the loss first: part-time income, a fallback job, or a small test project instead of a full leap.
3. **Respect bandwidth.** Do not ask for long-term discipline from people whose present is already overloaded. Attention is a resource, not a moral virtue.
4. **Understand why people mix caution with hope.** A family may buy a lottery ticket and keep a fixed deposit at the same time. That is not stupidity. It is a search for upside without letting the floor disappear.
5. **Treat small experiments as insurance.** The more fragile the household, the more useful a tiny reversible experiment becomes.

## Avoid these traps

- **Confusing caution with laziness** - A person can be hardworking and still refuse a bet that could break the family.
- **Giving long speeches about future thinking** - Future thinking is easiest when today is already safe.
- **Mocking small dreams** - People often start with tiny, reversible moves because they have no luxury of failure.

## Quick reference

**Speak:**
- "What happens in the bad month?"
- "How much loss can this family absorb?"
- "Can we make this bet smaller?"
- "What would a protected version of this risk look like?"

**Avoid:**
- "Average return is all that matters."
- "They should just think long term."
- "Risk is good, so take more of it."
- "If they are careful, they are not ambitious."
